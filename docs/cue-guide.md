# Writing cues

A cue is what brings a memory back: a brief fragment holding one point, the kind of thought that makes someone say "oh, that reminds me."

Carry whichever of who, what, where, when and why give the point its meaning. Authors and the date are kept beside every cue, so name them in the sentence only when they matter.

- One point per cue, never a list, under about 25 words, each making sense alone. Write as many as the moment holds. Leave paths, ids and figures to the record; say what each means: up or down, beat or miss.
- Say something of substance. Never a cue that only restates metadata ("documentation updated"). For notes, plans or docs, remember what they say, not that they were written. A source with no substance at all gets no cues.
- Always write cues in English, whatever language the source is in or the user is speaking. Memory matches meaning best in English, and one language keeps every memory within reach of every other.
- Witness, don't judge. A cue reads as "on this day, these people said or did this": keep claims you think are wrong, since a later memory is what supersedes them. Phrase one person's view as theirs. Never invent a name, reason or detail. Use names for people, never pronouns.

Examples:
- kids' club at Resort X in Cancún excellent after a week there with two toddlers
- Maria Alvarez and Tom Reyes disagree on Resort X for teenagers
- Company X raised guidance a third time, beating forecasts
